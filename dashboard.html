6 Recommended Data Cleaning Methods & Statistical Justification

Methodology
Column: Age (Categorical Variable)
4,177 Missing (43.90%)

1. What is missing: Audience maturity rating labels (all, 7+, 13+, 16+, 18+).

2. Where it is missing: 4,177 movie rows across Prime Video (1,948), Netflix (1,797), Hulu (329), and Disney+ (197).

3. Why this cleaning method: Because 43.9% of movies lack an official MPAA certification, synthesizing artificial age ratings (such as mode imputation to 18+) would skew family demographics and falsely mark indie/family films. Imputing with an explicit "Not Rated" category preserves 100% of the sample without misrepresentation.

4. How it can be fixed: Map all empty/blank strings to "Not Rated" in data structures.

5. Expected Cleaned Value: "Not Rated"

Column: Rotten Tomatoes (Numerical Ratio)
7 Missing (0.07%)

1. What is missing: Aggregate critic score string formatted as score/100.

2. Where it is missing: Exactly 7 specific rows (Lines 3690–3696: stand-up comedy and digital kids stories).

3. Why this cleaning method: With only 7 out of 9,515 records missing (0.07%), median score imputation (52/100) is statistically robust. Median is preferred over mean (53.5/100) because it is resilient to extreme review outliers and preserves dataset rating distribution neutrality.

4. How it can be fixed: Impute the dataset median score "52/100".

5. Expected Cleaned Value: "52/100" (Median) or "53.5/100" (Mean)
